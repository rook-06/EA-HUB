# Skill: Offer Builder

Constructs Grand Slam Offers for any of Damien's businesses using the Hormozi framework. An offer built this way creates a category of one — it cannot be directly compared to competitors, making price the last thing the client considers.

*Always read `references/hormozi.md` before running this skill.*

## When to use

- Damien wants to attract more clients and isn't sure how to position his service
- A service is getting price-shopped or competing on price
- Damien is launching a new service or wants to repackage an existing one
- The Quote & Estimate skill needs to be elevated into a full offer

---

## Process

### Step 1 — Define the avatar and dream outcome

Ask Damien:
- Who exactly is the target client? (homeowners, local businesses, first-time tattoo clients, etc.)
- What do they actually want at the end? Not the service — the outcome. The feeling. The result.

Typical answers by business:
- ATN: "a yard that looks great without me having to think about it"
- Dametime Tattoos: "exactly the piece I've been imagining, done right the first time, with no regret"
- Dametime Marketing: "inbound clients coming to me without me having to do the marketing"

### Step 2 — Map every obstacle

List every friction point the prospect faces:
- Before they hire (finding someone reliable, not knowing the price, fear of commitment)
- During the service (will they show up? will the work meet expectations?)
- After the service (did it actually work? will they need to chase you? what if something goes wrong?)

Each obstacle is a revenue leak. Each solved obstacle becomes a named deliverable.

### Step 3 — Convert obstacles into deliverables

Flip every obstacle into a named, solution-oriented feature or bonus. Examples:

| Obstacle | Named Deliverable |
|---|---|
| "Will they actually show up?" | Guaranteed Arrival Window |
| "How do I know the work was done right?" | Before & After Photo Documentation |
| "What if I don't like the result?" | 60-Day Satisfaction Guarantee |
| "I don't know how to explain my tattoo idea" | Complimentary Design Consultation |
| "I don't know what I'm getting into" | Written Scope Confirmation Before Any Work Starts |

### Step 4 — Identify high-value, low-cost delivery formats

Use the Delivery Cube to find items that cost you nothing but feel valuable to the client:
- Text or photo follow-ups after a service: near-zero cost, high client comfort
- A written care or maintenance schedule: 15 minutes to create, retained value
- A defined check-in at day 7 or 30: a phone call, but signals attention and professionalism

### Step 5 — Build the Guarantee

Choose the right type for this offer:
- **Unconditional** (full refund): best when the risk is low or you're trying to break into a new market
- **Conditional** (refund if client does X and doesn't get Y): safest for Damien right now — sets expectations both ways
- **Performance** (pay based on results): highest conversion, reserve for Dametime Marketing where results are measurable

Rule: the guarantee should scare you slightly. That's how you know it's strong enough to matter.

### Step 6 — Stack and price

List all deliverables (core service + named bonuses + guarantee). Assign a defensible standalone value to each. Total them. Price the bundle below that total value. The gap between "stated value" and "actual price" is the psychological deal.

Trim anything that costs more than it's worth to the client. Keep anything that costs little but feels valuable. Cut padding — only include what actually moves the needle.

### Step 7 — Apply SUGBN enhancements

- **Scarcity:** Is there a real capacity limit? "Only 3 new recurring lawn clients per neighborhood" — only say this if it's true.
- **Urgency:** Is there a real time limit? Seasonal work, a price increase, a booking deadline.
- **Bonuses:** Presented after the client asks to move forward — not upfront.
- **Guarantee:** Already built in Step 5. Make sure it's visible and named prominently.
- **Naming:** Apply MAGIC formula. Example: "The Above the Norm Season-Long Property Care Program."

### Step 8 — Name the offer

Apply the MAGIC formula:
- M = Magnet (the hook — why now, why this?)
- A = Avatar (specific target client)
- G = Goal (specific outcome they want)
- I = Interval (how fast they get it)
- C = Container (a word that frames it as a defined thing — Program, System, Plan)

Avoid generic names. "Lawn Care Services" is not an offer name. "The Chicago Homeowner Curb Appeal Program" is.

### Step 9 — Present to Damien for review

Output the full offer as:
1. Name of the offer
2. Target avatar (1 sentence)
3. Dream outcome (1 sentence)
4. What's included (named deliverables with individual values)
5. Total stated value
6. Actual price
7. The guarantee
8. Scarcity/urgency (if applicable)
9. Suggested CTA or first line for outreach

## Quality standards

- Every item in the stack must have a specific, defensible value. "Priceless" is not a value.
- The guarantee must feel genuinely risky. If it doesn't, strengthen it.
- The offer must be something a prospect could not find elsewhere in the same exact packaging. If it sounds generic, it is.
- Read `references/hormozi.md` before finalizing any output.
