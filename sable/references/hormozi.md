# Hormozi Framework Reference

*Synthesized from $100M Offers, $100M Leads, $100M Money Models, and The Game podcast. Read this before any offer-building, outreach, pricing, content, or retention task.*

---

## The Value Equation

**Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) / (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)**

- Increase the numerator: what the client wants, and how certain they are they'll get it
- Collapse the denominator: how long it takes, how hard it is for them
- Done-for-you services score highest on effort; guarantees score highest on perceived likelihood; speed of first result attacks time delay
- If either denominator variable approaches zero, perceived value approaches infinite

## The Grand Slam Offer

An offer so specific and well-constructed that it cannot be compared to any other offer. Creates a category of one. Makes price comparison impossible.

**5-step construction:**
1. Identify the dream outcome (not what you sell — what they actually want)
2. List every obstacle the prospect faces before, during, and after your service
3. Flip each obstacle into a named, solution-oriented deliverable
4. Use the Delivery Cube to identify high-value/low-cost delivery formats
5. Trim & Stack: remove high-cost/low-value items; bundle remaining items as a stacked offer showing individual prices

**5 enhancements (SUGBN):**
- **Scarcity** — real supply limits (route capacity, time slots, cohort size)
- **Urgency** — real time pressure (seasonal window, price increase date)
- **Guarantees** — see below
- **Bonuses** — additive items, each named and valued separately; presented after the client asks to proceed to handle objections
- **Naming** — MAGIC formula (below)

**Guarantee types:**
- Unconditional — full refund, no questions (strongest for conversion, highest risk)
- Conditional — refund if client does specific things and still doesn't get results
- Anti-guarantee — all sales final (signals extreme confidence, works when demand is high)
- Performance/implied — you only earn when client gets results (revenue share)

**MAGIC Naming Formula:**
M = Magnet (reason or hook) | A = Avatar (who it's for) | G = Goal (specific outcome) | I = Interval (timeframe) | C = Container (program/system word)
Example: "The Winnipeg Homeowner Yard Transformation Program — look maintained all season, starting this week."

**Niching multiplier:**
Same core service — generic = $19, specific avatar = $1,997. Narrowing the target client raises perceived fit and perceived likelihood of outcome.

## The Core Four (Lead Generation)

**Sequence for a cash-strapped operator:**
1. **Warm Outreach** → first
2. **Free Content** → second
3. **Cold Outreach** → third
4. **Paid Ads** → last (only after a proven offer and working sales process)

**Warm Outreach Process:**
- List every person in your phone, social media, email who knows your name
- Message each one personally: "Do you know anyone who's dealing with [problem] and wants [result]?"
- Don't pitch — let them self-qualify or refer
- For local services: "Do you know anyone who's been meaning to get their yard sorted?"

**The Rule of 100:** Pick one channel. Commit to 100 units per day, every day: 100 outreach messages OR 100 minutes of content creation OR $100 in ads. No exceptions.

**Lead Magnets (3 types that work):**
1. Reveal Problems — diagnose something they didn't know they had (free property walkthrough, free marketing audit)
2. Samples and Trials — full but brief access to core service (free first mow, free design consult)
3. One Step of a Multi-Step Process — give the first step free; the rest require purchase (free rough tattoo sketch before booking)

**Cold Outreach:**
- Build a targeted list filtered by likely-buyer characteristics
- Personalize genuinely — reference their specific situation, not a template token
- Lead with real value upfront, not a generic PDF
- 8–12 touches before declaring a lead dead
- Respond to replies within 60 seconds when possible (391% conversion increase vs. 1 hour)

**Lead Getters (second-phase, after Core Four is running):**
- Customers (referral systems)
- Employees (lead generation built into their role)
- Agencies (third parties running marketing)
- Affiliates (complementary businesses who promote you for reciprocal benefit or commission)

## Content Strategy

**Philosophy:** Give away the secrets, sell the implementation. The people who will do it themselves were never going to hire you anyway. Publishing expertise pre-qualifies buyers.

**Hook / Retain / Reward framework:**
- **Hook:** Pain-first, under 3 seconds. Name the specific problem the viewer is already feeling.
- **Retain:** Keep them through storytelling, a list, or steps with a promised payoff still coming.
- **Reward:** The actual actionable insight. This is what makes people follow and return.

**Content Accordion (scaling without more hours):**
- Create one long-form piece → slice into 30+ platform cuts (Reels, TikTok clips, text posts, carousels, email snippets)
- Volume creates skill early on — start with high volume, iterate fast, quality improves through feedback
- 5-10 short-form posts per day is sustainable; feed-based platforms (Facebook, LinkedIn) cap at 1-2

**Platform logic:**
- YouTube/long-form: highest trust, highest conversion per viewer
- Instagram Reels / TikTok / YouTube Shorts: awareness and acquisition funnel
- Facebook: local reach for service businesses
- LinkedIn: B2B and business owner targeting

**70-20-10 content mix:**
- 70% core business topic
- 20% adjacent topics
- 10% personal content

**"Document, don't create":** For operators with no audience and no time — film what you're already doing. Before/after on every job. Process clips during sessions. Tip videos during downtime.

**Content is the lubricant, not the funnel.** Content builds trust at scale. Direct outreach (warm + cold) is still what closes jobs in the short term. Both run simultaneously.

## Pricing & Retention

**Virtuous Cycle (vs. Vicious Cycle):**
- Vicious: cut price → margin drops → service degrades → worse results → reviews suffer → cut price again
- Virtuous: raise price → more margin → better fulfillment → better results → clients stay and refer → justify higher price

**LTGP (Lifetime Gross Profit):**
- LTGP = Gross Profit Per Customer ÷ Monthly Churn Rate
- Recurring lawn care client: $200/mo × 60% margin = $120 GP ÷ 2% churn = $6,000 LTGP (not $200)
- This math should drive every retention and acquisition decision
- LTGP ÷ CAC should be at least 3:1 (6:1+ ideal for personal service businesses)

**The $100M Money Model sequence:**
1. Attraction Offer — acquire the customer at low/break-even cost
2. Upsell/Downsell — maximize transaction value
3. Continuity Offer — convert to recurring revenue (the most neglected stage for local service businesses)

**Churn curve inflection points:**
- First 30 days: highest risk (20%+ churn rate) — onboarding is the primary lever
- Month 3: second inflection (~10% churn)
- Month 6+: stabilizes at ~2%

**The 4Rs post-sale loop:** Retain → Review → Refer → Resell

**Churn reduction tactics:**
1. Structured onboarding (reconfirm decision, set expectations, create early win)
2. Engagement tracking (proactive contact if a client goes quiet)
3. Regular check-ins (every 14–21 days for first 90 days)
4. Exit interviews (never let a client cancel without a conversation)
5. Milestones and unlockables (tenure rewards increase psychological switching cost)

## Application by Business

### Above the Norm
- Sell a seasonal/annual maintenance program, not individual services (Continuity Offer)
- Dream outcome: "yard that looks maintained all season without thinking about it"
- Lead magnet: free property walkthrough and assessment (Reveal Problems model)
- Grand Slam enhancement: guarantee visible improvement by week 6 or last service refunded
- Scarcity that's true: "only accepting 3 new recurring clients per neighborhood"
- LTGP math: $200/mo recurring client is worth ~$6,000 over their lifetime at steady-state churn
- Before/after content on every single job — shoot it automatically

### Dametime Tattoos
- Dream outcome: "exactly the piece I've been imagining, done right the first time, without regret"
- Lead magnet: free consultation and rough sketch before booking
- Grand Slam enhancement: included touch-up guarantee (highest-leverage risk reversal, near-zero cost)
- Naming: position the booking process as "The Custom Piece Process" — not just an appointment
- Content: Hook = reference image or blank skin; Retain = in-progress shots; Reward = finished/healed piece
- Every caption has one CTA: direct to DM

### Dametime Marketing
- Don't sell "marketing services" — sell a specific outcome to a specific client type
- Example offer: "10 booked consultations in 30 days for home service businesses in Winnipeg, or we run month 2 for free"
- Dream outcome: predictable inbound leads without doing the marketing yourself
- Lead magnet: free 30-minute marketing audit showing exactly what's costing them
- Unfair advantage: Damien is the client type he serves — use that as explicit positioning
- Case studies: document what he does for ATN and Dametime Tattoos; these are the first case studies
- LinkedIn for business owner targeting; Instagram Reels for local small businesses
