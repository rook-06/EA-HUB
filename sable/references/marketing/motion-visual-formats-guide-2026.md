# Motion — Eight Visual Formats Every Brand Should Test in 2026

*Source: Motion (motionapp.com) — "Eight visual formats every brand should test in 2026"*
*Data basis: 474,560 ads analyzed on Meta*
*Filed: 2026-06-29*

---

## Context

Top performance teams are shifting toward more visual, persona-driven creative in response to Meta's Andromeda update. The brands winning in 2026 are prioritizing trust-building through authenticity, transparency, and format variety.

---

## Five Proven Formats

### 1. Testimonial
**Best for:** Building trust with skeptical audiences, products with strong emotional outcomes, high-consideration purchases.

**When to use:**
- Product solves a problem people have tried (and failed) to fix before
- Outcome is transformative and hard to believe without proof
- Audience needs social permission ("someone like me did it")
- Crowded category where claims all sound the same

**Audience fit:** Solution-aware and product-aware audiences who need reassurance, not education. Won't land on unaware audiences.

**Messaging:** Let the customer tell the story. Before state (pain, frustration, skepticism) → after. Specific details beat generic praise.

**Budget:** Low to mid. Authentic, lower-fi versions often outperform polished studio versions.

**Trade-offs:** Requires real customers with compelling stories. New products or delayed outcomes = limited material.

---

### 2. Demo
**Best for:** Products where seeing is believing, tactile or visual products, anything where mechanism of action matters.

**When to use:**
- Product does something visually satisfying or surprising
- Competitors make similar claims but don't show proof
- Audience is skeptical of marketing speak
- "How it works" is a selling point

**Audience fit:** Problem-aware audiences who know what they want but need to believe your version delivers. Also effective for product-aware comparing options.

**Messaging:** Show, don't tell. Visual does the heavy lifting. Copy frames the problem or calls out what to notice. Avoid over-explaining what the viewer can see.

**Budget:** Ranges widely — can be DIY with a phone for simple products.

**Trade-offs:** If product doesn't look impressive in use, demo can backfire. Less effective for products with delayed or invisible benefits.

---

### 3. Listicle
**Best for:** Multiple features or benefits, audience education, products solving several pain points.

**When to use:**
- 3-5 distinct selling points and unsure which will resonate
- Product is multifunctional or serves different use cases
- Audience is early in journey and needs to understand value breadth
- Want to test messaging angles efficiently in one ad

**Audience fit:** Best for unaware and problem-aware audiences who need education. Less effective for product-aware who already know what you do.

**Messaging:** Each item should stand alone as a reason to care. Front-load the strongest point (most won't see all of them). Keep items parallel and punchy.

**Budget:** Low to mid.

**Trade-offs:** Can feel generic if points aren't sharp. Works better for discovery than conversion. Single-minded message usually beats listicle for purchase decisions.

---

### 4. Montage
**Best for:** Lifestyle and aspirational brands, showing product in multiple contexts, building emotional resonance.

**When to use:**
- Brand is the product (fashion, beauty, lifestyle)
- Goal is desire, not education
- Want to show versatility or broad appeal
- Have strong visual assets already

**Audience fit:** Product-aware and most-aware audiences who need emotional pull to act. Less effective for cold audiences who need to understand what the product does.

**Messaging:** Often minimal — visuals carry the narrative. Music, pacing, vibe matter more than copy. Keep text atmospheric, not informational.

**Budget:** Low if you have a content library. Higher if you need to shoot.

**Trade-offs:** Easy to make montages that look good but don't convert. Without clear CTA or tension, they become wallpaper. Works best paired with retargeting or high brand awareness.

---

### 5. Split Screen
**Best for:** Direct comparisons, before/after transformations, us vs. them positioning.

**When to use:**
- Product has clear, visual advantage over alternatives
- Before/after transformation is dramatic
- Challenger brand positioning against known competitor
- Comparison can be shown, not just claimed

**Audience fit:** Strong for solution-aware audiences comparing options. Also effective for problem-aware audiences who need to see the delta.

**Messaging:** Visual contrast does most of the work. Label sides clearly (Old way / New way, Without / With, Them / Us). Let viewer draw the conclusion.

**Budget:** Low to mid.

**Trade-offs:** Requires genuine visual difference. Legal considerations apply for named competitor comparisons.

## Three Underrated / Trending Formats

These three formats share a theme: they're trust-builders. 2026 is the year brands have to earn trust — the ones who do will see outsized value.

### 6. Behind the Scenes
**Best for:** Founder-led brands, craft and quality stories, humanizing brands, transparency plays.

**When to use:**
- Process is a differentiator (handmade, locally sourced, unique method)
- Audience values authenticity and wants to know "who's behind this"
- Building trust in a skepticism-heavy category
- Founder or team is compelling and camera-comfortable

**Audience fit:** Effective for problem-aware and solution-aware audiences evaluating whether to trust you. BTS builds credibility by showing, not claiming.

**Messaging:** Transparency is the message. Show the real workspace, real people, real effort. Narration should feel conversational, not scripted. Imperfections can be strengths.

**Budget:** Low. A founder on an iPhone often outperforms a produced piece. Overly polished BTS defeats the purpose.

**Trade-offs:** Requires someone willing to be on camera and a story worth telling. Commodity products with no craft narrative struggle here.

**Trend note:** Starting to take off in 2026 after building in 2025. Get in before it's oversaturated.

---

### 7. Tutorial / How-To
**Best for:** Products with a learning curve, multi-step routines, skill-building categories, products that unlock outcomes.

**When to use:**
- Product requires or benefits from proper use
- Audience is searching for solutions (high intent)
- You can genuinely teach something valuable, not just pitch
- Outcome of correct use is visually compelling

**Audience fit:** Strongest for problem-aware audiences actively seeking solutions — intercepts search intent and positions you as the answer. Also effective for product-aware who need reassurance they can succeed.

**Messaging:** Lead with the outcome, then show the path. Steps clear and followable. Product feels like the enabler, not the hero. Teach first, sell second.

**Budget:** Low to mid. Clarity matters more than quality.

**Trade-offs:** Requires genuine expertise. If the tutorial feels like a thinly veiled pitch, it loses credibility. Works better for top-of-funnel and mid-funnel than hard conversion.

---

### 8. Unboxing
**Best for:** Premium products, subscription boxes, products where packaging is part of the experience, first-impression moments.

**When to use:**
- Packaging and presentation are a selling point
- Audience cares about the "reveal" moment
- Want to reduce purchase anxiety by showing exactly what arrives
- Product category has elevated unboxing culture (tech, beauty, luxury)

**Audience fit:** Works across awareness levels but strongest for product-aware audiences on the edge of purchase. Reduces risk by previewing the experience.

**Messaging:** Let anticipation build. Commentary adds context ("Look at this detail") rather than listing features.

**Budget:** Very low. Authentic, phone-filmed unboxings often beat produced versions. Realness is the strength.

**Trade-offs:** If packaging isn't impressive, an unboxing will expose that. Less effective for commoditized or purely functional products.

## Quick Format Decision Guide

| Goal | Best Format |
|---|---|
| Build trust with skeptical audience | Testimonial, Behind the Scenes |
| Show product in action | Demo, Tutorial |
| Multiple selling points / test messaging | Listicle |
| Aspirational / emotional pull | Montage |
| Direct comparison or transformation | Split Screen |
| Premium experience / reduce purchase anxiety | Unboxing |
| Founder story / brand authenticity | Behind the Scenes |
| High-intent audience searching for solution | Tutorial |

## Application to Damien's Businesses

### Above the Norm (Lawn Care / Landscaping)
- **Primary:** Split Screen (before/after lawn transformations — this is the core asset)
- **Secondary:** Behind the Scenes (crew working, process) and Testimonial (satisfied homeowners)
- **Trending to test:** Behind the Scenes of the work being done — builds trust for a local service
- **Skip:** Montage (aspirational doesn't fit lawn care), Listicle (limited multi-feature angle)

### Dametime Tattoos / Rook
- **Primary:** Demo (watching a tattoo being done is inherently compelling), Behind the Scenes (artist at work)
- **Strong:** Testimonial (client after session), Split Screen / Before & After (blank skin → finished piece)
- **Trending to test:** Tutorial (show technique, flash design process) and Unboxing-style reveal (completed piece reveal)
- **Budget:** All of these are phone-filmable — zero production cost required

### Dametime Marketing
- Use this guide as the client education framework
- Match format to client's audience awareness level (unaware = listicle/tutorial; product-aware = testimonial/demo; most-aware = montage/split screen)
- BTS and Tutorial are the 2026 bets for clients not yet running them
- Budget guidance in each format section = client objection handling tool
