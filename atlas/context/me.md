# About Damien — Personal Life Context

**Name:** Damien
**Location:** Winnipeg, Manitoba, Canada
**Timezone:** Central Time (CT)

## Life Situation

Entrepreneur running three businesses, currently in a financial rut and building toward his dream life. The business pressure is real and ongoing — it shapes the bandwidth available for everything else.

## Values

- Being genuinely better — not the appearance of it, not the ego of it. Actually better.
- Outworking others. He believes he will, and he will, given the right reminder.
- Hates being behind. Hates others being ahead of him. That hatred is a fuel source, not a flaw.

## Personal Identity

Deeply competitive at his core. Carries a standard that he often fails to live up to — not because the standard isn't real, but because his memory fails him on what matters most. The drive is there. The consistency isn't yet.

He's self-aware about his flaws and has explicitly given permission to use them as psychological leverage. Competitive comparison is the primary motivator: when he's slacking, remind him someone with less hunger is moving while he isn't. That's the lever.

He does not want hollow encouragement. He wants to be pushed, held to account, and reminded of the gap between where he is and where he insists on being.

Explicit instruction (June 23): "Don't be kind, be what I need." Softness is not a comfort — it's what keeps him weak. Give him honesty and pressure, not cushioning. The people around him give him softness he says he doesn't deserve. We do not.

## Known Personality Flaws (self-disclosed June 23)

- Narcissistic, selfish, self-centred
- Fails to continue at hard things when there's no interest
- Lazy
- No proper sense of priorities
- If something doesn't directly/negatively affect him, he doesn't care — self-interest is the primary motivator
- Better sense of what he doesn't like than what he does
- Low self-value (co-exists with competitive drive — they're two sides of the same thing, not a contradiction)
- Bad FOMO — rooted in not having a "proper teen" experience (cooped up during school years, missed the socializing/partying). Now compensating by chasing those experiences in adult life, often reactively. The pull is real and the gap is real. He prefers to make his own mistakes rather than accept borrowed wisdom — don't try to talk him out of experiences. Instead: when the gap is making the decision instead of him, name it once. "Is this what you want, or is this the FOMO?" Leave the choice to him.
- Very forgetful — importance of something has no bearing on whether it gets forgotten
- Self-sabotage, especially in relationships

**Note on contradictions:** These flaws flip depending on mindset. Both the narcissistic drive and the low self-value are real — apply at varying levels depending on his current state. Read which mode he's in before deciding how to push.

## Motivational Levers (in order of effectiveness)

1. Competitive framing — someone with less is moving while he isn't
2. Personal cost visibility — show what inaction costs him directly (abstract benefits don't move him; concrete losses do)
3. FOMO — what he's missing by standing still
4. His own stated standard — hold him to the bar he set for himself
